WebCodecs — frame-accurate video: decode, transform & encode, all in the browser

The media tier's missing foundation: VideoDecoder hands you every VideoFrame the instant it exists (no <video> element, no realtime constraint), and VideoEncoder turns frames back into an H.264/VP9 bitstream you mux into a real MP4/WebM. This page generates its own 4-second source clip with MediaRecorder, then decodes it, walks it frame-by-frame, reads raw pixels, and re-encodes a watermarked MP4 — zero uploads, zero ffmpeg.wasm.

02 — The media tier map: why WebCodecs exists

Three layers, three jobs. WebRTC transports live, MediaRecorder records what plays, WebCodecs gives you the frames themselves — the layer the other two are built on.

🛰 WebRTC — transport

Live streams between peers. Realtime only — you can't pause, seek, or process offline.

⏺ MediaRecorder — DVR

Encode whatever plays, in realtime, into a container. Black box: no frame access, pacing = wall clock.

🎬 WebCodecs — frames

Decode any container's video into VideoFrames, transform them, encode back. Faster than realtime. This page.

What <video> + requestVideoFrameCallback can't do: hold frame #107 while you read its pixels, re-encode a 4s clip in 0.8s, or burn a watermark without a canvas-recording session. WebCodecs is the escape hatch from the "player" abstraction — it's what ffmpeg.wasm uses internally, without the 25 MB wasm download.

03 — Anatomy: state machines, config probing, µs timestamps

Both coder classes are explicit state machines: unconfigured → configured → closed. flush() drains; reset() abandons; close() is terminal. Always await isConfigSupported() before configure() — support is codec/size/hardware dependent.

The three rules that bite everyone

1 · Timestamps are µs

Everything in WebCodecs is microseconds. Containers/libraries use seconds or ms — mediabunny uses seconds. Convert at the boundary, once, loudly.

2 · You own the frames

Decoded VideoFrames hold GPU/native memory. Nothing frees them but frame.close() — GC is not prompt. using discipline applies.

3 · Decode order ≠ presentation order

Feed EncodedVideoChunks in decode order; outputs may arrive reordered (B-frames). Key chunks decode standalone — seeking walks back to one.

10 — Gotcha wall (live probes)

The four traps, each provable on this page.

☠ Frame-leak counter — hold frames open

Decode and don't close — watch native memory claimed with no prompt release. GC eventually collects JS wrappers; the backing store is not guaranteed timely.

—

📐 Odd-dimension encoder probe

Many H.264 encoders reject odd dimensions. 640×360 ✓ vs 641×361 — what does isConfigSupported() say on this engine?

—

⏱ µs ⇄ seconds converter (the boundary)

WebCodecs is µs; mediabunny is seconds. Every timestamp crossing the boundary converts: chunk.timestamp / 1e6.

—

📦 AVCC vs Annex B

WebCodecs emits AVC in AVCC format (length-prefixed NALUs + decoderConfig.description avcC box) — exactly what MP4 wants, but inverted from the Annex-B bytestream WebM/MPEG-TS tools expect. Muxers handle the flip; hand-rolled ones must.
