CSS Multi-column — the fragmentainer tier

The screen fragmentainer: column math, balancing, the fill-auto void, the silent clip, spanners, the WebKit geometry void, fragmentation and the snap-per-column reader — live-probed on Chromium 149 + Firefox 151 + WebKit 26.5, every claim re-measured on your engine below.

§1The column math

Columns divide the content box: N columns of (W − gap×(N−1))/N. With only column-width, the used count is max(1, floor((W+gap)/(cw+gap))) — never zero. column-gap: normal computes normal but behaves as 1em of the multicol's own font. Column rules paint centered in the gap and consume no layout.

§2Balance vs sequential fill — and the fill-auto void

Under a height constraint, balance and auto agree while content overflows (both fill sequentially, extra content marches into overflow columns). They differ when content fits: balance spreads it across all columns, auto stacks it in the first. With no height constraint at all, column-fill:auto collapses to ONE column on Chromium/Firefox but keeps balancing on WebKit — the fill-auto void.

§3The overflow-column law, the silent clip & the wheel-axis trap

A height-constrained multicol that runs out of room doesn't grow down — it grows right: overflow columns paint past the content box, scrollWidth balloons. Wrap that in overflow:hidden and the extra columns become unpainted and unreachable — the silent clip. With overflow:auto the content is reachable but only horizontally — and a plain vertical wheel scrolls nothing at all, on every engine.

wrapper overflow:hidden — columns 4+ exist, invisible
wrapper overflow:auto — scroll me with your wheel ↓
silent clip (paint truth) wheel-axis trap (scroll the pane) overflow:auto reaches the tail

§4The snap-per-column reader

§5The spanner tier

column-span: all breaks the multicol into rows: content above and below balances into its own column rows, the spanner takes the full content width — universal. But a spanner nested inside a wrapper diverges: Chromium parks the wrapper in the first column, Firefox in the last, and WebKit makes the wrapper itself span. Margins are honored on Chromium/Firefox.

§6The WebKit geometry void

On WebKit 26.5, getBoundingClientRect() — and everything built on it: offsetTop, scrollIntoView math, popover anchoring, e2e rect assertions, DevTools inspect — reports a constant wrong offset (−146px, column 0) for items in the row above a spanner, for every item except the last. The paint is fine. The hit-testing is fine. Only the geometry lies. This is the modern signature of the 2020 "Safari duplicates content above column-span" bug family. Chromium and Firefox report exact.

spanner-adjacent rects exact paint & hit-test still true

§7Fragmentation: avoid-cards, the oversized block, break-before

break-inside: avoid keeps cards whole — 9 cards land 3/3/3 on every engine, and mixed-height balancing came out byte-identical across all three engines (the "engines balance differently" folklore measured false on this bench). But an avoid-block taller than the column gets its own column and its tail clipped at the column bottom — and on WebKit its reported column is wrong while it paints in the right one (the §6 void, unbreakable edition). break-before: column lands exactly at a column start everywhere. Orphans/widows: no effect on screen, any engine, any value.

§8The straddler's union rect

An element that fragments across columns reports its union box: width = the full multicol content width (both columns + the gap — even where the element has no fragment), height = first-fragment top to last-fragment bottom. Range client rects give the per-fragment truth. Pointer events and elementFromPoint hit every fragment correctly on every engine — the fragmentation is real to hit-testing, only the bounding box is a lie for measurement code.

§9The context tier: flex collapse & intrinsic widths

A column-width multicol sized by its content contributes one column: in a flex row its max-content basis is a single column wide, and the content stacks down that one column instead of fanning out. width: min-content collapses to the longest unbreakable token — a 27px column. column-count multicol keep their count everywhere. Give the flex child a definite basis, or the columns quietly vanish.

sibling takes the rest
flex auto-basis = one column min-content = longest token

§10Rotation: RTL mirrors, writing-mode turns the axis

In RTL the first column is the rightmost and overflow columns extend into negative space on the left (scrollLeft range stays positive 0..max in an element scroller, all engines). In writing-mode: vertical-rl the column axis rotates: columns stack top→bottom, content reads right→left inside each. Both universal.

dir=rtl · first column right
vertical-rl · columns stack down
RTL first column at the right vertical-rl rotates the axis

§12Molt — the client multicol audit (12 checks, half a day)

  • HIGH — fixed-height multicol inside any clipping ancestor. Columns past the third exist in scrollWidth, paint nowhere, can never be reached: silent content loss (§3). Every "why is half my article missing" ticket.
  • HIGH — column-fill: auto on auto-height containers. One column on Chromium/Firefox, three on WebKit (§2). If the design says columns, test the void.
  • HIGH — geometry APIs near spanners on Safari. Rects, offsetTop, scrollIntoView targets, anchored popovers, e2e assertions — all report −146-style offsets for spanner-adjacent content while painting correctly (§6). QA sees "impossible" failures.
  • HIGH — e2e clicks/assertions on straddling elements. The bounding rect is the union box — width includes gaps and foreign columns (§8). Assert with Range rects or per-fragment reads.
  • HIGH — unbreakable blocks taller than the column. Own column, clipped tail on Chromium/Firefox; misreported placement on WebKit (§7). Cap card heights or allow breaking.
  • MED — column-width multicol as a flex child without a definite basis. Collapses to one column (§9).
  • MED — horizontal-only multicol scrollers for mouse users. A plain wheel scrolls nothing on any engine (§3); ship pagination or snap (§4, §11).
  • MED — nested spanners inside wrapper components. Three-engine wrapper split (§5); keep spanners as direct children.
  • MED — orphans/widows as a screen fix. No effect in continuous media on any engine (§7); use text-wrap or restructure.
  • MED — RTL overflow assumptions. Overflow columns extend left; positive scrollLeft range (§10). Don't port LTR scroll math.
  • LOW — balance-divergence expectations. Measured identical across engines on this bench — don't chase pixel-diff fails that aren't there.
  • LOW — animating column-count. Integer-stepped (2→3→4) with a full rebalance each step (§2 note) — fine for reveals, never for precision.
