§2THE SOFT-LIGHT SPEC DRIFT — every engine ships the legacy curve

The current W3C Compositing spec defines soft-light with a smooth piecewise curve (the (16x−12)x+4 polynomial). No engine implements it. All three ship the legacy PDF-era piecewise formula, byte-identical to each other — off from the spec formula by up to 23/255 in a single channel. Any design tool, shader or parity test that computes the spec formula disagrees with every browser.

§3THE PLUS FAMILY — plus-lighter everywhere, plus-darker WebKit-only

§4THE ISOLATION CENSUS — 34 wrappers, one question: does the blend stop?

A blue multiply child inside a wrapper inside a red parent. If the wrapper isolates, the row renders blue (child blends only with the wrapper's transparent group, then composites normally). If not, the row renders #2f0c12 (child blends through to the red grandparent). Engine-uniform on every one of the 34 variants — this matrix renders live in your engine; the tags are the recorded 3-engine verdicts.

The headline traps: identity values isolate — transform:translate(0,0), rotate:0deg, scale:1, filter:blur(0px), opacity:0.99 are visual no-ops that silently stop blending. And position:sticky isolates even before it ever sticks. Meanwhile overflow:hidden, border-radius, display:flow-root and column-count isolate nothing.

§5THE BLEND-TARGET LAW — and the negative-z backdrop skip

The backdrop of a blended element is everything painted below it inside the nearest isolated group: it walks through unlimited transparent ancestors (the grandparent's background blends), includes earlier same-level siblings, reaches the root canvas (both body and html backgrounds). It does not include later siblings at z-index:-1 — negative stacking levels are skipped by the backdrop entirely, then the opaque blended result paints over them.

1 · blend walks to grandparent (parent transparent)

2 · earlier sibling joins the backdrop (green × blue)

3 · later sibling at z-index:-1 is SKIPPED (page bg blends instead)

§7THE ORDER LAWS — filter, opacity, clip

Filter on the blended element applies BEFORE blending (source filtered, then blended); filter on an ancestor applies AFTER the group composites (the child blends with the unfiltered group content, then everything is filtered together). Opacity follows the compositing formula (1−α)·backdrop + α·blend — not a fade of the blended result. Clip applies to the source before blending.

§8THE PLUS-LIGHTER CROSSFADE — the no-dip dissolve

Two half-opacity layers: under source-over the stack loses light at every intermediate value (the classic opacity cross-fade dip); under plus-lighter the layers add premultiplied — 0.5·red + 0.5·blue = (255,128,255) over white, brighter than source-over's (128,64,191) by exactly the under-layer's contribution. This is the primitive for flicker-free cross-fades (and what engines were supposed to interpolate blend modes with — see §10).

§9THE NO-ANIMATION LAW — blend modes snap

transition: mix-blend-mode 800ms is accepted by every engine — and the mode flips instantly at t=0. Pixel-sampled at 15% of an 800ms multiply→screen transition, the render is already byte-exact screen on all 3 engines. No 50% discrete flip, no plus-lighter cross-fade, no warning. Animate opacity instead (the α-law of §7 keeps the blend live at every value).

Click: the color changes on the same frame the style flips — the 800ms duration is a no-op. Verified by pixel sampling at t+120ms across 3 engines: multiply #2f0c12 → #d576a2 = screen, no intermediate value ever rendered.

§10background-blend-mode — the own-layers cousin

Blends the element's own background layers with each other (image list × color), never the page beneath — byte-identical result over white and over green on all engines. Comma lists repeat per layer. Notably it accepts no plus-lighter/plus-darker on any engine. CfT serializes the computed list truncated to the layer count; Firefox/WebKit keep the authored list.

own layers multiply → #2f0c12 (same over any page bg)

over a green page: identical — never blends below

§11THE FRAME WALL + grammar

Iframe content blends with the iframe's own backdrop only — never across the frame boundary into the parent page (byte-exact on all 3: the child multiplied with the frame's red body, not the green host). Grammar: an invalid value drops the whole declaration (→ normal); values are ASCII case-insensitive; isolation initial auto, invalid → auto.

§13Molt audit — the client blend audit (12 checks, half a day)

  1. HIGH — hero duotones inside any wrapper that got a "harmless" transform, filter, will-change or opacity<1 for perf/GPU hints: the blend silently stops at the wrapper (§4 identity-isolation trap).
  2. HIGH — transition: mix-blend-mode or JS-tweened mode strings: instant snap on every engine, presented as a "transition" in review (§9).
  3. HIGH — decorative z-index:-1 layers (stripes, glows) expected to show through blended overlays: skipped by the backdrop on all engines (§5).
  4. HIGH — plus-darker in shared CSS: no-op outside Safari, silently normal (§3).
  5. HIGH — soft-light parity tests/screenshots against design tools that compute the W3C formula: every browser is the legacy curve, deltas to 23/255 (§2).
  6. MED — opacity used to "fade out" a blended overlay: result follows (1−α)·bd + α·blend, not a fade of the blended color (§7).
  7. MED — filter+blend order assumptions: filters on the element run before blending, filters on ancestors after — "my filter changed the blend color" is the order law (§7).
  8. MED — background-blend-mode expected to tint content behind the element: it never blends below (§10).
  9. MED — sticky headers/toolbars that carry blended children: sticky isolates even before sticking (§4).
  10. MED — embeds expected to blend with the host page: the frame wall stops it both ways (§11).
  11. LOW — QA on white pages: multiply over white is the identity — the fixture that hides every blend bug (method lesson).
  12. LOW — e2e asserting glyph pixels of blended text: ink coverage is font-dependent per engine; assert on flat-color fixtures (probe lesson).

Method. 5 probe batteries (~40 lanes × 3 engines) over a plain-HTTP loopback origin, fresh document per lane, verdicts by page.screenshot + pngjs pixel sampling at fixture centers (blending is pure paint — rects, scrollWidth, gCS cannot see it; gCS lanes are the one DOM-truth family). Expected values computed in-node from the W3C formulas; classification thresholds ±6/255; every "identity-looking" lane re-run with non-associative mode pairs (difference/screen) and colored page backgrounds after two self-ambiguating fixtures were caught.

Falsified this session (3 own): "plus-lighter at half opacity gives un-premultiplied add" (engines add premultiplied — my expectation was wrong, engines byte-identical) · "position:absolute/relative isolates" (no z-index → no isolation; the first census run's z0/zneg/sticky rows collapsed to 0-height wrappers — inset geometry is position-dependent, the margin-collapse sampling bug family) · "multiply over a white page discriminates blend states" (white is multiply's identity — the on/off lanes read identical; colored page backgrounds are mandatory in blend fixtures).

Gate lessons: (1) THE MARGIN-COLLAPSE SAMPLE SHIFT — row fixtures with symmetric margins collapse to a pitch of h+m, not h+2m: every sample point landed 14px low and every verdict shifted exactly one row; contiguous rows, zero margins, pitch = height. (2) THE IDENTITY-BACKDROP AMBIGUITY — white page + multiply = identity, and multiply composes associatively: three early lanes (mbb-self, z:-1 sibling, white-page QA) were self-ambiguating by construction; discriminating instruments = colored page backgrounds + non-associative mode pairs. (3) THE POSITION-VARIANT GEOMETRY COLLAPSE — variant styles that override position on inset-sized wrappers collapse them to zero height (inset needs position:absolute): the z0/zneg/sticky census rows measured the parent's bare background until wrappers got explicit width/height.
