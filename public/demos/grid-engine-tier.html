§3 · THE FLOOR LAW — 1fr is minmax(auto, 1fr) and the auto minimum is min-content

The #1 grid bug in production: a long unbreakable word (hash, URL, ID) in a card. The fr track refuses to shrink below its largest item's min-content — the grid overflows instead. This is the flexbox min-width:auto law's grid twin, and the standard fixes have a hidden split.

THE FLOOR LAW (3/3). grid-template-columns: 1fr 1fr with a 722px min-content item: track A = 722px, track B = 7px, container scrollWidth 729 in a 300px box. The fr share (150) is a maximum, not a guarantee.
THE FIX MATRIX (3/3). minmax(0,1fr) → 150/150, the token spills inside its box (paint-only overflow — pair with overflow:hidden). min-width:0 on the item → identical. overflow-wrap:anywhere → 150/150 and the token wraps (anywhere lowers the min-content floor itself). overflow-wrap:break-word → floor NOT lowered — same overflow as bare 1fr. Break-word only breaks when there is room to break INTO.

§5 · THE AUTO-STRETCH LAW — align-content:normal is stretch for auto tracks

Auto tracks don't sit at their content size when there's leftover space — they grow, and the growth is split equally, not proportionally. The "why are my columns different widths than their content" law.

EQUAL SPLIT (3/3). Contents 80.4 + 40.2 in 600px: free space 479.4 splits 239.7 each → tracks 320.1 / 279.9. Not proportional, not content-weighted. Any justify-content ≠ normal turns stretch off (80.4 / 40.2). fr tracks never stretch this way — they already ate the free space. Measured on the block axis too: auto rows 40/40 in a 240px grid → 120/120.
IMPLICIT TRACKS STRETCH TOO (3/3). 1 / span 5 past a 3-explicit grid creates 2 implicit auto tracks — they split the 300px leftover (150 each, measured in the serialization 100px 100px 100px 150px 150px). A runaway span silently becomes a stretched two-column appendix.

INTRINSIC CONTRIBUTIONS (3/3). A shrinkwrapped 1fr 1fr grid = 2 × its widest item (fr contributes max-content when sizing the container). A width:100%; max-width:340px item contributes exactly its max-content capped at 340 — the scrollbar-session "percentage-track inflation" mechanism: a 100%-wide scroll list inside a 1fr track sizes the track from the list's capped content, not the container. Nested grids propagate the same way: an inner grid's min-content floor (a long nowrap row) floors the outer 1fr — the flexbox nested-cascade twin, and the fix is the same (minmax(0,1fr) / min-width:0).

THE %+GAP LAW. Percentage tracks and percentage gaps in a definite container resolve against the content box and ignore each other: 50% 50% + any gap overflows. In an indefinite container both become content-based — and the % gap then overflows the content-sized box. Percentages in track lists are load-bearing invariants; audit them like currency conversions.

THE GHOST-LINE LAW (3/3). After an invalid template drops (gCS serializes none), the named lines it generated survive — and grid-area:a / grid-column:a-start items are NOT reflowed by auto-placement. They bind to ghost lines at the far end of an implicitly expanded grid: a 3-track 600px grid grew tracks 100px 100px 100px 144.86px 155.14px and the item landed at x=444.9 spanning the two stretched ghost tracks — on all three engines, byte-consistently, while the style readout said none. A template typo doesn't stack your components; it teleports them to a far corner and lies about it.

THE STATIC STACKING LAW (3/3). Overlapping grid items (lines/areas make overlap legal — it's a feature, not a bug) paint in DOM order, and z-index works on position:static items — verified by elementFromPoint hit-tests: later DOM wins by default; z-index:2 on the earlier item flips it; z-index:-1 sinks it below the auto sibling. Grid (like flexbox) is a layout mode where static elements are stacking participants. The flexbox Tab-order corollary applies: visual stacking ≠ focus order.

THE ABS-AREA LAW. For abs children, the grid area is a real containing block — when it is definite in an axis. §16 · The Molt client-site grid audit — 12 checks, half a day

Checks 1–5 pass every code review and ship broken; they are invisible without a long-token fixture or a narrow viewport.

  • HIGH — bare-fr card tracks: any repeat(auto-fill, minmax(N, 1fr)) with N ≠ 0 around user content (names, SKUs, hashes) — the minmax min protects the track, but hand-written 1fr 1fr split layouts don't have it (§3).
  • HIGH — 1 / -1 full-width assumptions: full-bleed rows inside a grid whose tracks don't fill the container — the span stops at the explicit grid (§9).
  • HIGH — auto-fit skeleton/loading states: 1–2 loaded items stretching to full width (§6); swap to auto-fill or clamp with max-width.
  • HIGH — auto-height shells with fr rows: 1fr rows equalize to the tallest sibling (§7) — "my rows are equal and I never asked" is this law, not a bug in your CSS.
  • HIGH — ghost placements after an areas refactor: a renamed/resized area leaves stale grid-area:name items teleporting to implicit corners while gCS says none (§11) — grep placements after every template edit.
  • MED — auto tracks growing: surprise equal-split growth under align-content:normal (§5); set justify-content:start or use fr to control it.
  • MED — percentage tracks + gap: 50% 50% + gap overflows by the gap (§8).
  • MED — % gap in content-sized grids: resolves after sizing and overflows (§8).
  • MED — dense packing with reordered DOM: dense + order/template edits fill holes far from the reading order — a11y divergence (§10).
  • MED — abs overlays with one-edge placement: percentages resolving against the container-end span (§13); write both edges.
  • LOW — scrollWidth overflow probes: Firefox reports grid overflow, CfT/WK clamp (§4) — use item rects in tests.
  • LOW — 0fr "collapsed" tracks: 0fr floors at min-content, not zero; minmax(0,0) is the real collapse (§4).
