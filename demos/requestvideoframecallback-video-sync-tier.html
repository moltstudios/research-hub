requestVideoFrameCallback — the video-frame sync tier

The render primitive that fires when a video frame is actually composited — not on the display clock (rAF), not on a UI-frequency timer (timeupdate). One method + one cancel + a 7-field metadata object. This page generates its own synthetic 30 fps mission video in-browser (zero network), then measures every contract live: the firing law, the rate law, the pixel law, the scrub law, the drift law.

01Engine introspection — the two-method surface

Live probes on the page you are reading. No events, no observers, no statics — requestVideoFrameCallback(cb) returns a handle id, cancelVideoFrameCallback(id) revokes it. Everything else arrives in the callback: (now, metadata).

The video-element-only law. The method lives on HTMLVideoElement.prototype — not on <audio> (it is undefined there even though audio is an HTMLMediaElement), not on <canvas>, not on document. Video compositor frames are the only trigger. Audio-frame sync needs the WebAudio clock or the mediaTime of a muted helper video.
No policy anywhere. No permission prompt, no secure-context requirement (present on plain http:// — probed on an insecure LAN origin), no Permissions-Policy directive (present in cross-origin iframes without allow=). The least-gated tier in the catalog alongside Network Information. The metadata is about the frame, not the user — nothing to fingerprint, nothing to gate.

The duration: null gotcha. MediaRecorder-produced webm lacks a duration header — video.duration reads null (Safari: Infinity) even after loadedmetadata. Seeking still works within the buffered range. Never gate your UI on duration being finite; derive it from a loadedmetadata + seekable probe, or remux with a duration-aware tool (ffmpeg / webm-duration-fix) before shipping real assets.

03LIVE — the firing law: one callback per composited frame

The whole tier in one sentence: the callback fires when a NEW frame is presented to the compositor — and only then. Below: the pre-play first-frame event, the play-through census, the paused silence vs rAF, and the ended stop. Runs automatically on load; replay any time.

The pre-play first-frame callback. Docs imply rVFC is a play()-driven loop. Measured: setting src and loading is enough — exactly one callback fires before any play(), when the first decoded frame composites. Useful (paint the poster frame to canvas for free) and surprising (your "playing" state machine may get an event before playback starts). Guard with video.paused or compare mediaTime, not with a started flag.
The hybrid loop law. While paused, rVFC is dead silent (0 callbacks in an 800 ms window) while rAF keeps ticking (~60 Hz). Video-driven canvas overlays must therefore be a hybrid: rVFC repaints on new frames; also repaint once on seeked (seek fires exactly one callback — §07) and keep any non-video animation in rAF. A pure-rVFC loop freezes on pause; a pure-rAF loop burns frames on dropped video.

05LIVE — the rate law: locked to the video clock, not the display clock

A 30 fps video on a ~60 Hz compositor fires at 30 Hz. The callback cadence is the video frame period — measured here during the play-through pass, middle third, ramp excluded. Then the stress test: playbackRate = 2 doubles the presented-frame rate, so the callback rate doubles with it.

min(video fps × rate, compositor) is the contract. rAF matches the display; rVFC matches the video. A 25 fps video on 60 Hz → 25 Hz callbacks; a 120 fps video on 60 Hz → clamped to the compositor. Measured here: 33.3 ms ± 0.1 inter-callback at 30 fps — a metronome, not a jittery timer. And under playbackRate=2: ~57 Hz. Per-frame work should sit here, never in rAF-while-video-plays (that redraws identical frames) and never in setInterval (that drifts against both clocks).

06LIVE — the pixel law: the composited frame IS the mediaTime frame

The falsification engine of this entry. During playback every callback samples a pixel of the video element itself (1×1 drawImage) and converts it to hue; the factory baked hue = (frame·3)%360. If metadata.mediaTime names the frame actually on screen, sampled hue must equal HUE(round(mediaTime·30)).

58/58 during play · Δ0° on seek — measured (probe battery). This is the license for frame-accurate overlays: anything you draw in the callback from a mediaTime-indexed table lands on the exact frame the human is seeing. It also falsifies the lazy alternative — a currentTime-indexed lookup draws the row of a frame that may not be on screen yet (§10's drift, made visible in §11's ghost HUD).

07LIVE — the scrub law: seek-while-paused fires exactly one callback

The trick nobody documents: on a paused video, currentTime = t composites the seeked frame — and rVFC fires once with that frame's metadata. Frame-perfect scrubbing UIs with no polling, no rAF loop, no seeked+drawImage guesswork.

08LIVE — the contracts: cancel, ids, multi-pending, loop, rate stress

The small print, measured: cancel is honored pre-fire and ignores bogus ids; two pending requests both fire; ids are monotonic; loop wraps mediaTime to 0 while presentedFrames keeps counting; 2× playbackRate doubles the callback rate.

The id contract. requestVideoFrameCallback() returns a monotonically increasing handle (observed mid-session ids 127, 128 — the counter does not reset per play). cancelVideoFrameCallback(bogus) is a silent no-op — no throw, no effect. Multi-pending: request twice before a frame lands and BOTH fire once. This is rAF's exact id/cancel grammar — the API was designed to feel native to rAF users.
Loop does not reset the counter. On wrap: mediaTime returns to 0, presentedFrames continues through the boundary (measured 182 → 188 across a loop). If you chart presentedFrames over time, loops are invisible — by design. Dedupe by mediaTime if you need "new content" semantics across a loop.

09LIVE — the timeupdate falsification

The "standard" way to sync UI to video is the timeupdate event. Measured during the same play-through: timeupdate fires at ~4.5 Hz — one event per ~7 frames. It is a UI-frequency clock, not a frame clock, and MDN says so if you read carefully: "15 to 250 ms".

6.7× resolution gap, measured. Any per-frame effect built on timeupdate (captions, telemetry overlays, waveform cursors) jitters by up to a quarter second. The three real clocks: rVFC for presented frames, rAF for display frames, WebAudio AudioContext.currentTime for audio-locked sync (the only clock that survives tab throttling with sample accuracy). timeupdate is for progress bars.

10LIVE — the drift law: mediaTime vs currentTime

Inside every callback, video.currentTime and metadata.mediaTime disagree slightly. Measured distribution across the play-through: currentTime typically runs ahead by ~3 ms (it is the playhead estimate; mediaTime is the presented frame's timestamp).

Lock overlays to mediaTime. Always. currentTime answers "where will the next frame be", mediaTime answers "what is on screen". At 30 fps a 3 ms lead is sub-frame — but at 60 fps, under seek, or on slower decode pipelines the lead crosses frame boundaries and the currentTime-indexed overlay draws the future. §11's ghost HUD demonstrates the wrong pattern live.

12CAPSTONE — Molt video-reactive hero: design that follows the footage

The design-agency use case: a hero whose accent color, glow and title treatment track the video behind it — per composited frame, zero WebGL, zero shaders. Each callback samples one pixel (1×1 drawImage), derives hue+luma, and writes CSS custom properties. rVFC means the effect updates only when the footage actually changes — free throttling.

14Ship checklist

#Rule
1Feature-detect once: 'requestVideoFrameCallback' in HTMLVideoElement.prototype — Baseline 2024, but fall back to rAF for the long tail
2Re-request at the END of the callback (rAF grammar); never setInterval the re-request
3Index overlays by mediaTime, never currentTime — the drift law (§10)
4Handle the pre-play first-frame callback: gate on video.paused/mediaTime, not a started flag (§03)
5Paused overlays: repaint on seek's single callback (§07); keep idle animation in rAF (§03 hybrid law)
6Watch ΔpresentedFrames > 1 — decode trouble; surface it, don't hide it
7Never gate on duration for MediaRecorder blobs — it reads null/Infinity (§02)
8Cancel with the returned id when unmounting; bogus ids are no-ops but stale callbacks leak work
9Respect prefers-reduced-motion: per-frame effects should freeze to a sampled still
10In CI: assert the contracts (pre-play=1, paused=0, seek=1, cancel honored, rate≈fps) — they are deterministic; do not assert exact frame counts of real-time recordings
