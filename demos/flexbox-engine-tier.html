§1 · The min-width:auto floor — flexbox's #1 shipped bug

A flex item with default min-width:auto refuses to shrink below its min-content width — the longest unbreakable word. The container overflows instead. Measured 3/3: a 334px word in a 300px container keeps the item at 334.2px and pushes the container to +43px scrollWidth.

THE FLOOR LAW. min-width:auto ⇒ the item's shrink floor is its min-content size (the widest word / widest atomic child). Deficits the layout can't pay inside the floor are paid outside the container: the row silently overflows. flex-grow does not help; flex-shrink cannot pass the floor.

THE MIN-WIDTH:0 SPILL CONVERSION. min-width:0 does not fix the overflow — it converts container-overflow into text-spill: measured 3/3, the box shrinks to 150px while the 334px word paints 184.2px past the box edge and the container still reports +34px scrollWidth. Pair min-width:0 with overflow:hidden (ellipsis) or a wrap keyword that lowers the floor (§2).
THE BREAK-WORD / ANYWHERE SPLIT. overflow-wrap:break-word is cosmetic for sizing — min-content stays the full word (item still 334.2px, container still overflows, 3/3). Only overflow-wrap:anywhere lowers the min-content floor itself: item 150px, container fits, text breaks inside. If your long-URL / hash / ID cell still overflows with break-word, this split is why.

§2 · The shrink arithmetic — weighted by basis × shrink

When the sum of bases exceeds the container, the deficit is distributed weighted by flex-basis × flex-shrink — not by shrink alone. Measured 3/3 to two decimals:

THE SHRINK FORMULA. lossi = deficit × (basisi × shrinki) / Σ(basis × shrink). Measured: bases 400/300 shrink 1/1 in 600 ⇒ 342.86 / 257.14; shrink 2/1 ⇒ 327.27 / 272.73; equal bases shrink 1/3 ⇒ deficit splits 25/75 ⇒ 325 / 275. flex-shrink:0 freezes the item and forces the sibling to absorb the whole deficit (300/300). Every result lands on the floor from §1 if it would pass below.

§3 · The grow arithmetic — and flex:1 ≠ flex-grow:1

Free space distributes weighted by flex-grow alone (basis plays no part): bases 100/200 grow 1/2 in 600 ⇒ 200/400, exact 3/3. The famous trap is the basis:

THE flex:1 ≠ flex-grow:1 LAW. flex:1 = 1 1 0% — basis zero, so the items split the container equally (150/150 content in 600 ⇒ 300/300, 3/3). flex-grow:1 alone leaves basis auto — items keep their content size and split only the leftover (same content ⇒ 225/375). Equal-looking card rows built on flex-grow:1 go unequal the moment content differs; built on flex:1 they stay equal but can starve small items.

BASIS vs WIDTH. In a row, flex-basis:200px beats width:300px (used 200, 3/3); flex-basis:auto defers to width. In a column the same holds against height. gCS reads the specified basis (auto stays auto — never materialized to the used width). flex-basis:content ignores width entirely (max-content wins: measured 250 vs 100). Defaults on every engine: 0 1 auto.

§4 · Auto margins eat the free space before justify-content sees it

The alignment people reach for and the one that silently wins are different properties. Measured 3/3, exact:

THE AUTO-MARGIN PRIORITY. Any margin:auto on a flex item absorbs the free space first; justify-content only distributes what's left — with auto margins present, that's nothing. Measured: justify-content:flex-end + margin-right:auto ⇒ item pinned at start (left 0); justify-content:center + margin-left:auto ⇒ item at end (340 of 400); two items both margin:auto under space-between ⇒ the pair is centered (space-between inert). The classic "push one item right" is margin-left:auto on the last item — and it works because of this law.

§5 · Stretch, the percentage child & the baseline family

THE STRETCH SET. Default cross alignment stretches an auto-sized item to the line (not the container, under wrap): measured 120 in a 120 container, 30 inside a 30px line. An explicit height beats stretch; margins subtract (10px vertical margins ⇒ 100 of 120). The percentage-child corollary: height:100% inside a stretched item resolves against the stretched size (120, 3/3) — it works even though the item's own height is auto; the same markup in a block parent collapses. Under wrap, stretch is capped by the line cross size (30, not 200) and default align-content:stretch grows the lines themselves (item became 90).
THE BASELINE FAMILY. align-items:baseline aligns first-line text baselines: a 28px-font item's box top sits 15px above the 14px item's (measured) while both baselines coincide; a <button> participates by its label baseline (top within 1–2px of the text item); an item with no text synthesizes its baseline from the bottom margin edge (spec) — measured: an empty 50px box parks its bottom edge at the text baseline, its top 35px above the text item's.

§6 · align-content grammar, per-line justify & safe center

THE ALIGN-CONTENT INERTIA SPLIT. align-content is inert on a nowrap container (start/center/end all ⇒ 0, 3/3) — but the moment flex-wrap:wrap is set it applies even with a single line: one 30px item in a 120px container moves 0 / 45 / 90 under start/center/end. "It's single-line, align-content can't matter" is false once wrap is on. And justify-content is always per-line: a full first line touches both edges (0–300) while a lone last-line item parks at line start — never redistributed across lines.
SAFE CENTER — Baseline 2026 across the trio. Overflowing items under justify-content:center spill both edges (−50 left, +50 right — unreachable content on the left in LTR). safe center falls back to start (0, all content reachable, 3/3); unsafe center ≡ plain center. Both keywords supported in CfT 149 / Firefox 151 / WebKit 26.5 — no prefix, no flag.

§7 · The feed matrix — overflow value × min-height (the OpenDrones law)

The vertical twin of §1, and the one that eats telemetry dashboards: a column-flex feed whose content is taller than the container. Six lanes, one matrix, measured 3/3:

THE FEED MATRIX. Feed is flex:1 in a 200px column with 1000px of content. visible (default): the item floors at content height (1000px), the footer is pushed to y=1030, container scrollHeight +856 — the classic crushed dashboard. overflow:auto / hidden / scroll: the automatic minimum is zeroed — the item takes its 144px share (200−header−footer) with no min-height:0 at all (spec §4.5: any overflow ≠ visible zeroes the auto-min). overflow:clip: does NOT zero it — still 1000px, still crushed (clip creates no scroll container; the sticky tier's clip-exception gains a flex-side corollary). min-height:0 alone: the box shrinks to 144 but visible-overflow content spills (+830 container scrollHeight) — the "I added min-height:0 and now content overlaps everything" variant.

The six-lane matrix, live

THE COLUMN-REVERSE ORIGIN LAW. flex-direction:column-reverse puts the scroll origin at the bottom: scrollTop reads 0 at load, the first DOM child sits at the bottom edge (top 340 of 400), scrolled-away content lives above in negative space (last child top −40), and appending never jumps — scrollTop stays 0 and the new row is in view. Plain column: scrollTop 0 at top, appends land below the fold, scrollTop never moves. This is the chat/telemetry pinned-feed pattern with no scroll juggling — measured 3/3.

§8 · order, real Tab order & z-index on static items

THE TAB-ORDER LAW (a11y headline). With orders 3/1/2 the visual row reads B,C,A — but real keyboard Tab (measured with trusted key events, 3/3 engines) visits A,B,C: DOM order. The spec forbids order from affecting sequential navigation, and all three engines obey — visual order and keyboard/screen-reader order permanently diverge after any order reflow. offsetLeft follows the visual order; nextElementSibling stays DOM; negative order pulls to the front. If a card grid is "in the wrong reading order", check for order before anything else.

THE STATIC STACKING LAW. z-index is honored on position:static flex items (a z:2 static item paints over a later-DOM z:1 — 3/3), and any z-index ≠ auto makes the static item a real stacking context: a grandchild z:99 inside a z:1 item stays under the sibling z:2 (measured via hit-test at the overlap). With z-index:auto the grandchild escapes above the sibling. Flexbox is the only layout mode where static elements stack — which is why "why is this static div layered" has a flexbox answer.

§9 · The engine splits & the misc matrix

THE COLLAPSE SPLIT (genuine 3-way). visibility:collapse on a flex item: the spec says it should remove the item from flex layout (like display:none for sizing). Measured: Firefox does (span 200 → 100, spec-correct); Chrome + WebKit keep the space exactly like hidden (span 200). The only flex property where the engines visibly disagree on this bench — never ship collapse-dependent layout.

THE RTL FAMILYdirection:rtl row and row-reverse each reverse the row (21); both together restore DOM order (12 — the double flip). scrollLeft 0 at the origin on all three.
THE AXIS ROTATIONUnder writing-mode:vertical-rl, row stacks top→bottom (inline axis) and column stacks right→left (block axis) — the direction names follow the writing mode, not the screen.
THE IMAGE STRETCH-RATIO LAWAn <img> with auto main-size stretches on the cross axis and re-resolves its main size through the aspect ratio: natural 80×40 stretched to 120 tall ⇒ width becomes 240; in a column it goes full-width 400 ⇒ height 200. align-items:flex-start restores natural size. The "my image exploded in flex" law.
ASPECT-RATIO + GROWaspect-ratio with default stretch: stretch wins (h=120). With flex-start: the ratio follows the grown width (150 ⇒ 75).
THE ABSOLUTE HYPOTHETICAL BOXAn abs child with inset:auto takes its static position from justify/align: center ⇒ (170, 50) exact in a 400×120 container; end ⇒ (340, 100); stretch doesn't move it (0,0). The alignment values leak into absolutely-positioned children.
THE NESTED CASCADEInner flex (300px content) as an item of a 200px outer flex: naive ⇒ outer overflows +100. min-width:0 on the inner ⇒ inner shrinks to 200 but its children now overflow it by +100 — the fix pushes the overflow one level down. Full fit needs shrinkability at every level (measured: inner 200, children 100/100, zero overflow).
INTRINSIC SIZING SPLITmin-content of a nowrap row = the sum of its items' floors (3×100 ⇒ 300); with wrap = the largest (100). A nowrap row never collapses for min-content sizing — grid-track and width:min-content parents get the sum.
ANONYMOUS ITEMSBare text nodes between element children become real flex items — gap applies across them (10px measured between a text item and a span) and they stretch/align like any item.
IGNORED PROPERTIESfloat, clear, vertical-align are inert on flex items (no float-up, same-row geometry, 3/3).
THE OUTER DISPLAYinline-flex computes as-is and stays inline-level outside; item defaults everywhere 0 1 auto; gCS flexBasis never materializes auto into the used width.

§11 · Method & engine ledger

4 probe batteries, ~95 measured lanes, one plain-HTTP loopback origin, self-calibrating floors (font-independent: expected values derived from in-page measured min-content twins, never hardcoded font metrics). Every lane builds its own fixtures; geometry read via getBoundingClientRect; paint order via elementFromPoint hit-tests at verified overlap points; focus order via trusted keyboard events (no synthetic events anywhere — the input-tier wall). Cross-lane contamination caught twice (document-absolute vs container-relative offsets; a probe point outside the overlap zone) and re-measured clean — both recorded in the entry's gate lessons.
