overscroll-behavior — the scroll-boundary tier

The property that promises to stop scroll chaining — wheel past the bottom of a chat pane, a map strip, a modal, and the page behind it stays still. Shipped everywhere since 2018 (Chrome 63 · Firefox 59 · Safari 16). This session measured what it actually does on three engines — and the promise silently fails on Firefox for wheel input.

Probed live in CfT 149 · Firefox 151 · WebKit 26.5 over a loopback origin with per-wheel position traces across ~60 lane-runs: nested element scrollers, the document viewport, iframes, keyboards, snap containers, RTL and vertical writing modes. The interactive labs below re-run the core lanes in your browser — wheel over them.

Catalog gap: 452 entries scanned — overscroll appears in 4 passing mentions, zero dedicated coverage. · Companion entries: Wheel tier · Scroll Snap · Smooth-scroll tier · RTL & Logical.

§1 · The chain grammar — what auto actually does

A scroller at its boundary hands the wheel to the nearest scrollable ancestor — the full delta, immediately. The lab below is a mini-document ("world") with a pane inside it. Wheel the pane to its bottom and keep wheeling: watch the world move under it.

THE CHAIN LAW. With auto, an at-edge scroller chains the entire wheel delta to the nearest scrollable ancestor — vertical and horizontal, element ancestors and the viewport — on every engine measured. The wheel that saturates mid-flight still loses its remainder (it vanishes — the wheel-tier law); the next at-edge wheel chains everything.

THE HANDOFF DEFERRAL (Firefox). Firefox never chains mid-burst: within an active wheel transaction the delta just dies at the edge; the chain happens on the next, fresh gesture (after ~2.5s idle or moving the pointer away and back). Chromium + WebKit chain on the very next wheel, inside the burst.

§2 · The transaction lab — contain, tested honestly

The classic test — "I put contain on the pane, I wheel to the bottom, the page doesn't move, it works!" — is contaminated: on Firefox, auto behaves identically mid-burst (§1, the deferral). You haven't tested contain until you've paused and wheeled again.

§3 · The target-selection trap — the first wheel

A pane that was script-scrolled to its edge (never user-scrolled) can be skipped by the first wheel entirely: the engine targets the root and the page jumps — contain can't help, because the wheel never targets the contained element. The engine bug class is Bugzilla 1595791 ("not honored on first interaction").

THE COLD-START LAW. On the bench, Firefox chained +400 on the very first wheel over a script-positioned, contained, at-edge pane (fresh page, no warm-up) — the wheel targeted the document, not the pane. One warm-up wheel over the pane (while it still has room) makes it the transaction target and the routing heals. Chromium/WebKit targeted the pane correctly on the first wheel. SSR/SPA panes that open pre-scrolled (chat logs, feeds) are the production shape of this trap.

§4 · The backdrop split — overflow:hidden + contain

The standard modal backdrop recipe: a fullscreen overlay with overflow:hidden; overscroll-behavior:contain. Per spec (and MDN), a scroll container with no overflow is "always at its scroll boundary" — so contain should stop the wheel. Chrome fixed this in 144 (Jan 2026). Firefox's fix (Bugzilla 1837436, landed 150) did not cover this bench's wheel path. WebKit never got it.

§5 · Keyboard chaining — the 144 fix, and the engines that never chain

§6 · Cross-frame chaining — contain on the child root

Wheel an embedded frame's content to its edge and keep going: the chain crosses the frame boundary into the host page — on all three engines. The only lever is overscroll-behavior on the child's html — and on Firefox even that does nothing.

§9 · The CSSOM tier — the two-value shorthand & the logical transpose

Auto-probed in your browser on load. Three surprises shipped here: the shorthand accepts two values (x y — undocumented in MDN's formal syntax), the logical longhands -block/-inline exist everywhere and transpose at computed-value time under writing modes, and a longhand-only declaration leaves the shorthand serializing as an empty string.

Lane limit: whether the root viewport behaviorally honors a body-level value when html is auto (the overflow-propagation rule) is untestable through the automation wheel pipeline — the viewport's overscroll affordances (pull-to-refresh, rubber-band, glow) don't engage headless. The none-vs-contain affordance difference is spec-documented but equally unmeasurable here: on this bench the two values are behaviorally identical for chaining on every engine.

§10 · The bulletproof lock — contain + one line of JS

Until Firefox gates wheels and Safari gates no-overflow containers, a CSS-only lock is a two-engine lock. The cross-engine recipe is contain plus a non-passive wheel guard on the overlay. This modal ships it — wheel it as hard as you like.

/* the cross-engine scroll lock (this page's §10 modal) */
overlay.addEventListener('wheel', e => e.preventDefault(), { passive: false });
// + keep overscroll-behavior: contain for the engines that honor it
// + focus-trap / Esc handling per the focus tier
WHY preventDefault IS SAFE HERE. The listener rides the overlay element — the wheel tier's passive wall only silences root-target listeners; element listeners default to non-passive in every engine measured. The guard costs nothing when contain works and saves the page when it doesn't.

§11 · OpenDrones capstone — the telemetry modal & the map strip

Two shipped surfaces, two laws applied:

  • The mission-detail modal — the guarded pattern from §10: contain (Chromium/WebKit) + the JS wheel guard (Firefox/Safari) + focus trap. Wheel the telemetry list to its end; the map behind it never moves. The same modal opened from a fleet table (the anchoring tier's row-loss lane) keeps its scroll lock through prepends.
  • The map strip / filmstrip — a horizontal pane at its end chains sideways into the page on fresh gestures (Firefox) or mid-burst (Chromium/WebKit). overscroll-behavior-x: contain stops it on two engines; the guard covers the third. Never rely on "horizontal doesn't chain" folklore — it was my own over-drive artifact, falsified in §7.

§12 · The Molt client audit — 12 checks

Half a day with any modal/pane-heavy client site. Checks 1–4 pass every code review and bleed in production on at least one engine.

  1. HIGH — contain-only modal locks: open the modal, wheel its body to the end, pause 3s, wheel again — on Firefox the page behind scrolls (§2). Ship the JS guard.
  2. HIGH — overflow:hidden + contain backdrops: same test on Safari — the page scrolls (§4). Chromium-144+-only as CSS.
  3. HIGH — pre-scrolled panes (chat/feed logs restored to bottom): the first wheel can jump the page outright on Firefox — the pane was never the wheel target (§3).
  4. HIGH — embedded third-party frames (maps, players): wheel at the child's edge scrolls your page on every engine; child-root contain is unreachable for embeds you don't own (§6).
  5. MED — keyboard bleed on Chrome-family: focused pane + held arrows scroll the page behind (144+ respects contain — apply it).
  6. MED — phase-A-tested containment claims: any e2e/QA note saying "contain works on Firefox" that only wheeled mid-burst — retest with the idle pause.
  7. MED — horizontal carousels without ob-x: strip ends chain into the page sideways on every engine (auto).
  8. MED — snap containers assumed self-contained: snap owns landings, not boundaries — chains past the last point (§8).
  9. LOW — root PTR/rubber-band expectations: html-level none is for pull-to-refresh/glow affordances (touch platforms) — desktop wheel tests can't verify it.
  10. LOW — two-value shorthand assumptions: it parses and serializes, but MDN's grammar says single-value — feature-test before shipping contain none.
  11. LOW — gCS propagation checks: body-level values never surface in html's computed style — don't build runtime detection on computed reads (cf. the scrollbar tier's Firefox wall).
  12. LOW — contain as a "performance" fix: it gates chaining, nothing else — it does not stop overscroll paint effects from compositing on engines that ignore it for input.

Method

Seven probe generations (gen1–gen7, ~60 lane-runs) over Playwright 1.61.1: CfT 149.0.7827.55 · Firefox 151.0 · WebKit 26.5 (pw build), launched headless with route-fulfilled fresh documents per lane. All scroll claims through the REAL input pipeline (mouse.wheel, keyboard.press) — synthetic events drive nothing (the standing wall). Per-wheel position traces (pane + ancestor + document sampled after every wheel with settles), self-calibrating saturating panes, verified at-edge preconditions printed in every verdict (the over-drive lesson), idle-break (≥2.6s) and pointer-away transaction breaks, same-origin iframe children with in-child max verification, CSSOM census on detached probes + a live vertical-rl probe.

Falsified on the bench this session (including two of my own)

  • "contain stops scroll chaining" — false on Firefox for wheel input (the headline).
  • "contain works on overflow:hidden backdrops" — Chromium-only (144+); spec-true, engine-false on FF/WK.
  • (mine) "Chromium/WebKit never chain the x-axis to the viewport" — an over-drive artifact: the drive wheels had already scrolled the document to its edge before the measured wheel. Re-measured with saturating panes: full x parity. The gen4 conclusion died in gen6.
  • (mine) "Firefox honors contain after warm-up" — the warm-up only opened the wheel transaction; the deferral did the blocking. The idle-break discriminator (gen4 TB) flipped it: fresh wheels chain through contain.
  • "contain differences are testable with a quick wheel" — the phase-A trap: auto and contain are indistinguishable mid-burst on Firefox.

Lane limits

Pull-to-refresh, rubber-band elasticity and overscroll glow are platform input affordances that do not engage through the automation wheel pipeline (headless macOS) — the none-vs-contain affordance split is spec-documented, unmeasured here. Touch chaining untested (no touch pipeline). The behavioral transpose of -block/-inline under vertical-rl was probed at the CSSOM level only (computed-value mapping measured 3/3); the chained-geometry lanes need a human-hand pass — the mirrored-axis fixtures defeat clean automation reads (two attempts recorded honestly). Firefox's synthesized-wheel scroll quantum measured ~0.6× the issued delta on this bench (300 issued → ~180–221 applied); chained amounts are reported as applied, not issued.
