hidden=until-found — the reveal tier

The engine's own "collapse now, reveal on search" primitive: the content-visibility mechanism, the attribute-removal reveal, the window.find three-way split, the findability matrix, beforematch grammar, fragment + scroll-to-text paths, the override grammar, the loading law and the a11y face — live-probed on Chromium 149 + Firefox 151 + WebKit 26.5, every claim re-measured on your engine below.

§1The mechanism law — content-visibility, not display

Every engine implements hidden=until-found as UA content-visibility: hidden. getComputedStyle().display reads block — the lie every "is it hidden?" check falls for. The element keeps only its own padding/border box (an empty visible strip if it has chrome), children keep full layout boxes (rects are real!) but get no paint, no focus, no tree entry.

  • The truth instruments: getAttribute('hidden'), offsetHeight, getComputedStyle().contentVisibility — never display.
  • bench 3/3 identical on Chromium, Firefox, WebKit: cv:hidden · display:block · offsetH = padding+border only.

§2The IDL enum grammar — el.hidden is not a boolean anymore

The IDL getter returns true | false | "until-found". The string setter works: el.hidden = 'until-found' collapses, el.hidden = false reveals by attribute removal. Invalid enum values degrade to the hidden state. Type any attribute value and watch the parse:

§4THE WINDOW.FIND SPLIT — search vs reveal (the automation headline)

window.find() exists on all three engines and searches until-found content everywhere — but the reveal happens on Firefox only. Chromium scrolls to the invisible match and leaves a selection Range inside content you cannot see; WebKit does not even scroll. The real find bar (human hand) is the supported reveal path per BCD.

§5The fragment law — the universal reveal path

Hash navigation into until-found content reveals on every engine: beforematch fires, the attribute is removed, the page scrolls — at load time, after load, and via real anchor clicks. §6The override grammar — what beats until-found

Falsified folklore: author display:block does nothing (the hiding isn't display). The real override lever is content-visibility: visible, which un-hides from the start. Author display:none wins permanently — until-found can never reveal it (the custom-accordion trap).

§7THE UPPERCASE TRAP + the stateful find cursor

hidden="UNTIL-FOUND" is styled as until-found on all 3 engines — but Chromium's find will not search it (styled-hidden AND unfindable: the worst quadrant), while Firefox finds + reveals and WebKit finds. Author CSS [hidden=until-found] never matches the uppercase form (selectors are case-sensitive) even though the engine does. And window.find is a stateful cursor: consecutive calls advance through occurrences.

  • Case grammar: find('ALPHA') vs find('alpha') — the search is case-sensitive and the aCaseSensitive argument is ignored (3/3). aBackwards and wrap are honored (3/3).
  • THE ONE-CURSOR LAW: one strictly-forward cursor serves the whole find session across string changes — every find matches only occurrences AFTER the previous match (any needle); a needle earlier in the page returns false until you pass aBackwards or aWrapAround. Failed finds do not advance the cursor. Run multi-needle lanes in DOM order.

§8beforematch — the grammar and the reveal log

bubbles:true · cancelable:false · composed:false — you cannot veto a reveal. It fires on the until-found element while still collapsed (the handler sees the pre-reveal height — the settle-your-UI window), innermost-first for nested sections. It does not fire for fragment-opened <details> — until-found reveals only.

§9The loading law + the focus/a11y wall

until-found is a reveal state, not a lazy loader: images and iframes inside are fetched, loaded and decoded while hidden (3/3 — pair it with loading=lazy/c-v:auto for budget). Content is unfocusable and absent from the accessibility tree while hidden (CDP AX-tree verified); both restored on reveal.

§11The Molt audit — the Ctrl+F audit (12 checks, half a day)

Checks 1–5 pass code review and ship invisible to search.

  • HIGH 1 — display:none accordions/FAQs: the #1 "Ctrl+F can't find my content" ticket. Migrate to hidden=until-found (free reveal + beforematch).
  • HIGH 2 — visibility:hidden panels: laid out but unfindable (3/3). Same migration.
  • HIGH 3 — tab UIs hiding inactive panels with display:none: searchable tabs only by accident; until-found panels make every tab searchable.
  • HIGH 4 — until-found defeated by author display:none: the custom-accordion trap — until-found can never win against it (3/3). Audit every ancestor, not just the section.
  • HIGH 5 — framework re-render re-collapsing revealed sections: the reveal is an attribute removal a vdom will "repair" — reconcile it or own the attribute in state.
  • HIGH 6 — hidden UI relying on find: opacity:0/hidden-styled content below the fold is unfindable (3/3) — in-viewport invisible is found, far-below-fold invisible is skipped.
  • MED 6 — template whitespace in the attribute ("until-found " → dead collapse, silent).
  • MED 7 — e2e find lanes expecting reveal from window.find (Firefox-only; use the fragment path).
  • MED 8 — scroll assertions after find on WebKit (find does not scroll there; fragments do).
  • MED 9 — content-visibility:hidden sections assumed searchable (never, 3/3 — until-found is the findable variant).
  • MED 10 — hidden attribute typos in the enum (uppercase = Chromium-unfindable; anything invalid = plain hidden).
  • LOW 11 — "is it hidden?" checks reading gCS display (reads block; use offsetHeight/attribute).
  • LOW 12 — hidden-matchable cargo cult (content-visibility:hidden-matchable parses nowhere today — CSS.supports false 3/3, computes to visible; until-found replaced it).
  • LOW 13 — getSelection() reads right after find (empty for ~250ms on Firefox; permanently empty into invisible content on cft/wk).

§13Method, falsifications, lane limits

  • Method: 4 probe generations + a diagnostic pass (~45 lanes × 3 engines), find-lane fixtures scrolled into view first (the offscreen-invisible wall), plain-HTTP loopback, fresh document per lane, settle windows ≥250ms after every find/hash mutation, rAF double-settle before geometry reads, request-logged server for resource lanes, CDP AX-tree for the a11y face.
  • Falsified on the bench: "until-found is display:none + flip" (it is UA content-visibility) · "author display:block disables until-found" (no-op — the fix is content-visibility:visible) · "window.find triggers the reveal" (Firefox only) · "uppercase ≡ lowercase attribute" (styled yes, find no on Chromium) · "c-v:hidden / visibility:hidden content is searchable" (never) · "invisible content is findable anywhere on the page" (only in/near the viewport — opacity:0 below the fold is skipped 3/3) · my own "ff/wk lose findability after re-arm" (the forward cursor) · my own "switching strings restarts the search" (it does not — ONE forward cursor across string changes; my re-arm probe's needles happened to be forward-ordered) · my own "images inside don't decode" (invalid PNG fixture — the uniform-wrong-fixture family).
  • Lane limits: the real find bar (Ctrl+F UI) cannot be driven by automation on any engine — its reveal behavior on Chromium/WebKit is per BCD "supported" but human-hand-verified only; Playwright's WebKit carries no proprietary-codec constraints here (no media lanes); the ff beforematch-without-reveal quirk on a failing same-string re-find is recorded as-is (1 observation).
  • Gate lessons (this session): (0) THE INTERLEAVED-AUTORUN CURSOR STEAL — every window.find on a page shares ONE forward-only cursor for the page's lifetime: an auto-run lane scheduled mid-way through another find lane silently advanced the cursor past the remaining rows (the det row failed on every engine until the lanes were strictly serialized). Schedule find-bearing lanes strictly after the previous completes. (1) THE DISPLAY LIE IN YOUR OWN CHECKS — every hidden/revealed assertion in this demo reads offsetHeight + the attribute, never gCS.display (the entry's own §1 law applied to its own instruments). (2) THE CURSOR-CONTAMINATED FIND LANE — two window.find calls on the same string in one document advance the cursor: find-lane fixtures need a UNIQUE needle per button press. (3) THE FIND-SCROLL SIDE-EFFECT — window.find scrolls the page it runs in: every demo lane restores scroll after its find or the next section's below-fold chips measure the wrong viewport.
