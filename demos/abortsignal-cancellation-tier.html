1 · Surface census — the smallest tier in the catalog

AbortController has exactly two members: abort() and the signal getter (same instance every read). AbortSignal has five: aborted, reason, onabort, throwIfAborted() + constructor. Three statics: abort(), any(), timeout(). It IS an EventTarget — that's the whole eventing story. reason before any abort is undefined. The proposed canAbort()/progress()/passive() extensions: absent. new AbortSignal() is an illegal constructor.

2 · THE REASON LAW — what abort() actually hands you

THE LAW. abort() with no argument (or literally undefined) synthesizes a fresh DOMException named AbortError per controller — engine-worded: "signal is aborted without reason" on Chromium, "This operation was aborted" on Node. Anything else — string, null, Error, plain object — becomes signal.reason by reference, verbatim. null IS a reason (distinct from undefined!). And the once-law: a second abort() is completely ignored — the reason is never replaced.

This is the law prior entries kept rediscovering: fetch rejects with your string verbatim (Encoding/WS entries), EyeDropper's {signal} rejects with the raw reason not a DOMException, WebOTP's blast radius carries your reason across every pending get. One primitive, one contract — finally probed on its own.

3 · The event grammar — sync dispatch, onabort last

THE ORDER. abort() dispatches synchronously: every addEventListener listener runs during the abort call (registration order), then onabort, then your next line of sync code — the microtask queue never gets involved in delivery. The event: type:'abort', bubbles:false, cancelable:false, isTrusted:true. Capture-phase listeners fire too (same-target both-phase law). Duplicate registrations dedup (EventTarget law). And late listeners never fire — there is exactly one abort event per signal, ever.

The abort reason on expiry is a DOMException named TimeoutError — not AbortError — message "signal timed out" (Chromium) / "The operation was aborted due to timeout" (Node). That name is your error-UI discriminant: err.name === 'TimeoutError' → show "took too long", AbortError → show "cancelled".

THE NO-CANCEL LANE. A timeout signal has no controller — you cannot cancel the timer, only ignore it. Finishing early does nothing; listeners keep the signal GC-rooted until expiry (MDN's own guidance: for long timeouts, prefer setTimeout + clearTimeout + a real controller). Timers measure active time — paused in bfcache/suspended workers. Timeout signals still fire with zero listeners attached.

5 · any() — the composition tier

THE WIRING. AbortSignal.any([a, b]) — composite aborts the instant any parent aborts, with that parent's reason verbatim. One-directional: the composite never aborts its parents, parents stay independent siblings. any([]) = a signal that can never abort. A pre-aborted parent makes the composite instantly aborted (reason inherited) — even when the composite is wired after the fact. Nested any() chains propagate reasons through every level. Garbage parents: any([null]) → TypeError: Failed to convert value to 'AbortSignal'.

The watchdog pattern: any([AbortSignal.timeout(N), userCtrl.signal]) — either the clock or the user kills the operation, and the rejection reason tells you which one fired.

6 · throwIfAborted() — the sync guard

Live signal → returns undefined, no throw. Aborted → throws your raw reason — string, object, Error, whatever you stored. This is the checkpoint primitive for long synchronous phases (parse loops, encode loops) that can't await: for (…big work…) if (i % 1000 === 0) signal.throwIfAborted(). Distinct from the aborted boolean: one is a query, the other is a tripwire.

7 · fetch integration — & THE BODY-PATH REASON DIVERGENCE

THE DIVERGENCE. On the fetch promise, the reason-verbatim law holds everywhere: reject with your exact reason, mid-flight or pre-flight. On the body path it is version-dependent: Chromium 148 loses your reason and synthesizes a default — reader: AbortError: BodyStreamBuffer was aborted, res.text(): "The user aborted a request." — while Chromium 154 propagates the raw reason verbatim in both lanes (Node 25.8: verbatim). The chips above show what your current engine does. Production rule: branch on signal.aborted, never on rejection identity across the body path.

Also live above: abort-after-settle is a silent no-op (nothing throws, ever), an aborted signal reused for a new fetch rejects instantly with the stored reason, and already-read chunks stay in your buffer after the cut — the evidence you collected before the axe fell is yours to keep. Probe-server log verified the pre-aborted lane never opens a socket.

THE DEAD-LISTENER LAW. Registering a listener with an already-aborted signal silently registers nothing — zero events, ever, no warning. And one aborted signal retro-cleans every target it was attached to. Page-teardown in one line: one controller, every addEventListener in the module opts in via {signal}.

9 · Streams — pipeTo under the axe

Live: an in-page ReadableStream that would enqueue twice, piped into a sink with {signal}, aborted between chunks. pipeTo rejects with the reason verbatim (both Chromium lanes + Node agree here — unlike the fetch body path), the sink keeps everything already written, and the source is cancelled — no dangling enqueues. Pairs with the Encoding entry's demand-gate lesson: TextEncoderStream has no signal lane; you gate demand with pulls, not aborts.

TEN RULES. 1 · Give every abort a reason — default AbortError is anonymous. 2 · Reasons are by-reference: strings/enums beat messages. 3 · Once only — second abort() is a no-op, don't build reset logic on it. 4 · Listeners are one-shot: re-arm means new controller. 5 · timeout() can't be cancelled — clearTimeout needs a real controller. 6 · Branch on signal.aborted for body-path handling, not rejection identity (≤148 engines lose it). 7 · TimeoutError ≠ AbortError — route them to different UI. 8 · One teardown controller per page/module; every listener opts in with {signal}. 9 · Don't string-match messages: Chromium, Node and Firefox all word them differently. 10 · The census before the code: check the API actually takes a signal (WS and EventSource don't).
