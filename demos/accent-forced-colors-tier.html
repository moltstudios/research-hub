accent-color × forced-colors — the interplay tier

What actually happens to the accent-color property (and the oklch grammar beneath it) when a user forces colors onto your page. Live-probed on Chrome for Testing 149, Firefox nightly 1532 and WebKit 26.5 via Playwright emulateMedia({forcedColors:'active'}) — pixel-sampled quarter-points, ~63 lane-runs. Companion to the Forced Colors Tier (the forcing layer itself) and the form-control styling tier (the accent matrix unforced).

§1Your real state — nothing here is simulated

The chips read your actual rendering state. Most visitors see none. If any read active, your OS is forcing colors onto this page right now — and every table below describing "forced" lanes is live for you.

Flip it without Windows: Chromium/Firefox DevTools → Rendering → Emulate CSS media feature forced-colors. In CI: Playwright page.emulateMedia({ forcedColors: 'active' }) — the exact lane that produced every "measured" claim below. One instrument warning learned the hard way: emulateMedia persists unmentioned keys across calls — always pass the full state, or open a fresh page per mode.

§2THE DEATH LAW — accent-color is ignored, not adapted

The hopeful folklore: forcing "darkens" or "simplifies" your accent. Measured: under forced colors, every accent value paints the same palette color — the property loses all meaning for that user.

Nine checked boxes: auto, #0075ff, #7629fe, #d30000, #00873d, #1a1a1a, #f0f0f0, #9a9a9a, oklch(0.62 0.25 145). Unforced you should see distinct fills. Flip forced-colors on and count: one color on CfT and Firefox, nine on WebKit (the no-op law, §3).

§4THE SYSTEM-COLOR SURVIVORS — accent-color: Highlight

One accent value survives forcing: a system color. accent-color: Highlight parses and paints on all three engines, and follows the user's palette when it remaps.

The lane

Under forcing on CfT: the Highlight-accented box remaps with the palette (#37336d), the purple one dies to the uniform fill. On Firefox even this survivor dies — gCS reads auto and the box paints black: Firefox discards the accent declaration entirely under forcing, system colors included.

System colors, live on your engine

gCS reads + painted swatches. Unforced they resolve to your platform defaults; under real HCM they resolve to the user's palette — the same keyword your checkbox would ride.

The recipe: accent-color: Highlight is the only authored accent that (a) never fights the forced layer on CfT, (b) already matches what the UA paints anyway, and (c) costs nothing unforced. The complete inverse: never encode brand meaning in the accent value itself.

Gate-refined nuance (FF): Firefox honors fca:none on author-styled boxes — a plain <div class="led" style="forced-color-adjust:none"> keeps its background under forcing — while native control widget interiors (the radio's UA-painted disc) still force black. The hatch is real on FF for your own boxes; it just can't reach inside UA widgets. The a11y line, inherited from the pair entry and re-iterated because this session makes it sharper: fca:none opts a subtree out of the user's accessibility demand. Reserve it for brand islands (logos, status LEDs) that carry no information the user needs recolored — never for form controls whose checked state IS the information.

§7THE gCS GRAMMAR — oklch round-trips, then dies in the reflection

The accent grammar is modern-CSS-complete unforced — and the forced layer makes gCS actively lie about it.

Input (setProperty)Your engine, unforced gCSCfT forced gCSFF forced gCS

Grammar laws (3/3 engines, unforced): oklch round-trips as written (case-insensitive, bare components fine) · oklch(none 0.25 145) round-trips with the none-component · relative oklch(from red l c h) computes to a concrete color in gCS while staying unresolved in el.style (the serialization session's law, accent edition) · invalid values fall back to auto silently · initial and removed both read auto.

The audit trap: on CfT and Firefox under forcing, gCS reads auto for every accent — a JS audit comparing gCS against authored tokens reports "all accents missing" for exactly the users whose rendering you can't see anyway. WebKit — the engine that doesn't force — is the only one whose gCS stays honest under the forced MQ. Never gCS-diff accent-color in a forced-colors audit lane.

§9THE GLYPH + THE CONVERSION — oklch's one traceable descendant

Two small laws that close the loop: the check glyph flips with the palette, and oklch accent leaves exactly one measurable descendant — its sRGB conversion.

oklch → sRGB, live

swatch

The glyph law

On CfT the forced check glyph reads white on the light-palette fill (#37006e) and black on the dark-palette fill (#1aebff) — the UA inverts the glyph to keep contrast inside the user palette, exactly as it does for native selection UI. Firefox's glyph vanishes into the black fill on the sampled points. Never bake glyph-color assumptions into copy or e2e asserts: the glyph is palette-owned.

The conversion law: every engine flattens oklch(0.62 0.25 145) to the same sRGB #00a800 for accent purposes (and swatch paint) — a green, not the purple intuition suggests of "lightness .62 chroma .25", because hue 145 in oklch IS green. The one number an oklch accent contributes to forced-adjacent debugging is its sRGB descendant; the oklch form itself never survives any boundary (gCS grammar keeps it unforced, forcing drops the whole property, canvas never sees it).
